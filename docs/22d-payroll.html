65  Payroll and Pensions

Last updated

23 October 2026

A State Water Resources Department pays about nine hundred employees every month. Salaries are usually the largest expenditure of such a department, larger than all its purchases. After retirement the State pays them a pension, often for twenty or thirty years, and then a family pension to the spouse. So one wrong entry in a master is repeated every month, sometimes for decades. A clerk who never existed draws a salary in one office. A pensioner who died years ago is still paid, because nobody asked for the life certificate. Each shows only when the payroll is read against another record.

Earlier chapters already hold many payroll and pension tests, such as the pay bill rule book of Chapter 31 and the pensioners found dead in Chapter 36. This chapter runs them together for one department, in a few lines each. It then adds what the book lacks. These are a review of changes in the employee master, a check of the payroll against sanctioned strength and attendance, and a set of pension tests. Finally it combines the flags for each employee and pensioner, and follows two cases to the end.

As in every chapter of this part, a flag is not a finding. Many flags in payroll have an innocent cause, such as leave, deputation or an order entered late. The aim is to find the few that need a question.

65.1 Where payroll and pensions go wrong

Pay and pension pass through a few records, and each can be checked against the others.

The controls are simple. Nobody should be paid unless the person exists, holds a sanctioned post, is in service that month and is paid at a rate an order allows. A pensioner should be paid only while alive, and only what the rules give.

65.2 Our data, the payroll and pensions of a State water resources department

Our auditee is an imaginary Water Resources Department of a State, with a head office and eight division offices. We have its payroll and pension records for 2024-25. All names and numbers are made up. There are thirteen tables.

  • employees is the employee master, with dates of birth, joining and leaving, PAN, bank account and mobile number.
  • master_changes is its change log. It records each change of basic pay or bank account, with the order number and the user.
  • payroll has one row per employee per month. It holds the days paid, basic pay, dearness allowance (DA), house rent allowance (HRA), other allowances, deductions, net pay and the account credited.
  • arrears holds the supplementary bills for arrears, with the period covered.
  • sanctioned holds the posts of each cadre in each office, and attendance a monthly summary of days present and on leave.
  • pensioners is the pension master, with the pension payment order (PPO) number, type of pension, last pay, monthly pension and any date of death. A family pension carries the PPO of the pension it follows, in linked_ppo.
  • pension_paid holds the monthly payments, life_certs the life certificates and commutation the lump sums paid on commuting part of a pension.
  • bank_salary and treasury_pension hold monthly totals from the bank and the treasury.
  • oap is the welfare department’s old age pension list.

Pay follows a few example norms. DA is 50% of basic pay up to September 2024 and 53% from October 2024. HRA is 30%, 20% or 10% of basic pay in cities of class X, Y and Z. A service pension is half of the last pay drawn, and a family pension 30% of it, with a minimum of ₹9,000. Employees retire at the end of the month in which they reach 60. These are illustrative rules for this example only. In real work, apply the actual rules and rates of your government.

Most of the data is routine. Employees join mid-month and leave. Some increments were entered late and paid as arrears. A few employees were on long leave, and two engineers are on deputation to the head office. Some pensioners died and their spouses draw a family pension. A few pensioners are over a hundred, and some sent their life certificates late. Into this, these cases have been planted.

  1. Paid after leaving. A clerk of the Motinagar office who resigned on 31 August 2024 is paid for two more months.
  2. Paid before joining. A Junior Engineer who joined at Amarpur in January 2025 is paid for December 2024 too.
  3. A pay rise with no order. A clerk of Dhaulagarh has basic pay raised by more than a fifth, and the change log shows no order.
  4. Two increments in a year. A senior clerk of Belkheda receives a second annual increment in January.
  5. Arrears paid twice. The same arrears bill of one employee is paid again two months later.
  6. A bank change just before arrears. An accountant’s account is changed four days before an arrears payment, and changed back ten days after it.
  7. Ghost employees. Three clerks of Lakhanpur are paid every month but never attend. They share one mobile number.
  8. A very old pensioner without a life certificate. A pensioner over 100 years old is paid all year, though his last life certificate is from 2021.
  9. Family pension and service pension together. A pensioner died in July 2024. His widow is paid a family pension, and his own pension continues too.
  10. Commutation paid twice, and a pension well above the rule.

The tests of earlier chapters will find a few more. Let us build the data.

Two keys link the tables. emp_code links the employee master to the change log, payroll, arrears and attendance. ppo_no links the pension master to the payments, certificates and commutation. Every test below is a join along one of these links.

TipAudit tip, the data to request

Ask for the whole year, in raw form. For pay, that is the employee master with its change log, the monthly payroll with the account credited, the arrears bills, the sanctioned strength and the attendance summary. For pensions, it is the pension master, the monthly payments, the life certificate register and the commutation payments. The change log matters most. The current master shows only the last value, and hides a change made and then reversed. Ask also for the bank’s and treasury’s monthly totals. Note who ran each extract and when.

65.12 A suggested workflow

  1. Get the full data, including the change log, attendance, sanctioned strength and life certificates. Record who extracted it and when.
  2. Check completeness. Unique keys, every payee in the master, and totals agreeing with the bank and treasury.
  3. Review changes in the master. Pay outside service, rises without an order, two increments, bank changes near arrears, and arrears paid twice.
  4. Compare with the posts of each office and cadre.
  5. Compare with attendance, after fixing the keys. Separate those never recorded from those with a run of leave.
  6. Test the pensions. Life certificates, family and service pension together, commutation twice, the pension rule, and two lists.
  7. Add the tests of earlier chapters.
  8. Combine the flags for each person, and rank.
  9. Follow up the top cases on paper and in person, and record what cleared each flag.
ImportantRemember
  • Payroll is usually the largest expenditure of a department, and a pension is paid for decades. A small error in the master is repeated every month.
  • The change log shows what the current master hides, a pay rise without an order or a bank account changed and changed back.
  • Count the people paid against the posts sanctioned, and against the attendance. A ghost employee leaves a salary and nothing else.
  • Fix the keys before a join. A code that lost its leading zeros makes every employee look absent.
  • The life certificate is the main control on pensions. Test every payment against it.
  • Many flags have innocent causes, such as leave, deputation or a late entry. Several flags on one person deserve the auditor’s time.
